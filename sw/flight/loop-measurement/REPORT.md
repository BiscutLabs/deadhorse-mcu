# Flightory Stallion VTOL inner loop: measured RV32 instruction counts

Everything in the tables below is generated by `./run_all.sh` (about 30 s) from the sources in this directory;
`REPORT.md` is assembled by `tools/analyze.py` from `docs/REPORT_template.md` (hand-written prose) plus the generated
tables (`results/summary.md`). Numbers quoted in the prose were taken from the run of 2026-11-13 and are rounded.

## 1. Headline

* Fixed-point loop, rv32im, -O2 (config A): about 1.7k instructions per iteration in hover and fixed-wing and about 2.1k in
  mid-transition (worst mode), maximum single iteration seen 2171. That is **2.1 MIPS at 1 kHz (4.2 MIPS with 2x headroom)
  and 1.05 MIPS at 500 Hz (2.1 with 2x)**, instruction-count basis. The earlier 5,000-12,000 guesses are 2.4-5.7x too high for
  this loop content (see section 6 for why the loop content matters).
* No hardware multiplier (config C): about 32x more instructions (55-66k mean, 78k max), i.e. 66-78 MIPS at 1 kHz.
* Soft-float (config D): about 17x more (30-37k mean, 39k max), i.e. 37-39 MIPS at 1 kHz.
* rv32imc -Os: 3.8 kB of code for the loop and its helpers, 780 B state, 208 B stack, no libgcc helpers.

## 3. Loop content (this is my assumption, not the brief's)

The brief fixed the stage list; everything below that list is my design, written in the way a competent embedded engineer
would, and is the main source of uncertainty in the absolute numbers. Source: `src/control_step.c` (one source for both
numeric backends: `src/num_fixed.h` Q16.16 / Q2.30 with 64-bit intermediates, `src/num_float.h` single-precision float with
the same macro-level structure), parameters `src/control_init.c`.

* Loop rate: gains and filter coefficients designed for 1 kHz. Instruction counts do not depend on coefficient values, and
  running at 500 Hz only changes constants, so the same counts apply at 500 Hz.
* Actuators (9): 3 DShot motors (front-left, front-right, rear), 2 tilt servos, 4 control surfaces. **Assumed surface layout:
  2 ailerons (roll) + 1 elevator + 1 rudder.** No elevons, no V-tail, no flaps. Tilt servo range 1000..2000 us (mirrored on
  the right), surfaces 1500 +- 450 us, DShot 48..2047.
* IMU scaling: int16 minus int16 bias, times scale; gyro to rad/s (Q16.16), accel to g.
* Filters: per gyro axis notch biquad then low-pass biquad (direct form 1, Q30 coefficients, 64-bit accumulation), accel
  first-order low-pass. Filtered gyro feeds both the estimator and the rate loops.
* Estimator: Mahony quaternion filter (P + clamped I on the accel/gravity cross-product error, accel gated to 0.5-2 g,
  first-order quaternion integration, renormalisation). The accel normalisation inverse square root uses range reduction
  (x4 / /4), a linear initial guess and 2 Newton steps (about 0.4% error, no `clz`, no libgcc); quaternion renormalisation
  uses one Newton step from 1.0. In the float build the accel inverse sqrt is the bit-hack + 2 Newton steps (no libm).
* Attitude loop: conj(q) x q_cmd, shortest-path sign, P gain per axis blended between hover and fixed-wing by the transition
  variable, plus rate feed-forward, clamped to per-axis rate limits.
* Rate loop (hover): PID per axis, derivative on measurement with first-order D filter, integrator clamp with conditional
  integration (anti-windup), feed-forward on the rate setpoint, output clamped to +-1.
* Hover mixer: roll = front-left/right differential, pitch = front pair vs rear (collective preserving), yaw = differential
  front tilt, front thrust multiplied by 1/cos(tilt) from a 17-entry interpolated table, desaturation = scale the
  differential if it does not fit (one 32-bit divide), then shift collective (give up thrust before roll/pitch authority).
* Fixed-wing: same PID form with its own gains and state, output scaled by (Vref/V)^2 (one 32-bit divide) clamped to
  [0.4, 2.5], mixed to 4 surfaces with trim and gain.
* Transition: tilt schedule from a 17-entry interpolated table of the transition variable; linear blend of motors, tilts and
  surfaces. The hover controller is skipped when trans = 1, the fixed-wing controller when trans = 0 (so hover and fixed-wing
  mode are cheaper than a mid-transition iteration, which runs both).
* Output scaling: clamp, scale and round with integer clamps for 3 DShot and 6 servo values.
* Parameters live in the state struct in RAM (so the compiler cannot constant-fold gains), initialised by a non-measured
  `ctrl_init`.

Not included (brief exclusions or my choice): peripheral drivers, sensor FIFO parsing, RC decoding, navigation, GPS, arming
and failsafe logic, motor thrust linearisation, battery compensation, servo slew limits, dynamic/RPM notch tracking, gyro
bias estimation, logging, and any scheduler or interrupt overhead.

## 5. Reading the results

* Hardware multiplier: 19-20% of the instructions in config A are multiplies (mostly the mul + mulh pair of each 64-bit
  fixed-point product). Without the M extension (C) every one becomes a libgcc `__muldi3`/`__mulsi3` shift-and-add loop and
  the loop costs about 32x more, so RV32IM (at least the multiply) is not optional for a fixed-point build.
  `__muldi3` cost is data dependent, which is why config C has a much wider min..max spread.
* Fixed vs float on the same core without FPU: about 17x. Soft-float estimator + filters alone (about 15k) exceed the whole
  fixed-point loop 7x.
* Optimisation level: -Os costs nothing in instruction count here (within 1%, marginally fewer instructions than -O2) and saves 12.6% code; rv32imc -Os is 3.8 kB vs 5.6 kB for rv32im -O2. The C extension does not change
  the dynamic instruction count (46-48% of retired instructions are 16-bit).
* Per-stage (config A, inlined stages off): filters + estimator are about 40% of a hover iteration, the two rate/surface
  controllers about 11-19% each, output scaling 13%. In transition both controllers run, which is the roughly 21% increase over hover.
* Divides are rare (at most one per iteration in fixed-wing/transition; in hover only when the mixer must scale: 48 of 2,000 hover iterations in this scenario). A core with a slow iterative divider is therefore fine at these rates.
* Instruction count is not cycle count. The cycle-sensitivity table is an assumption-based illustration, not a measurement:
  even with a 3-cycle multiplier, 2-cycle loads and +2 cycles for each taken branch, the worst mode needs only about 3.4 MHz
  at 1 kHz, but the real CPI of the custom core is unknown to me.

## 6. Limitations, and what is solid vs approximate

Solid (measured, cross-checked three ways): dynamic instruction counts and category breakdowns for exactly this source, this
compiler (GCC 13.2.0), these flags and this scenario; code sizes; state sizes; the C/D/A ratios as they apply to this source.

Approximate:
* **Representativeness of the loop.** The loop is deliberately lean. A real firmware loop with e.g. a dynamic notch bank,
  motor linearisation, slew limiting, bias estimation, control allocation with a matrix, or larger rate/accel filtering will
  be larger. Rough unit costs from this data (derived, not separately measured): one extra biquad on 3 axes is about
  150 instructions (about 50 per biquad), one PID axis about 70, the Mahony update about 440. The loop content would have to grow roughly
  2.5x to bring config A to the low end of the earlier 5k guess. Treat 2.1k as a measured lower-middle value for the stated
  content, not a ceiling for a full flight stack.
* Maximum = maximum observed over 6,000 scenario iterations, not a proven worst-case execution time (branch coverage 85%,
  remaining edges cheap; see section 2).
* Stack: `-fstack-usage` frame of `control_step` plus the peak the simulator observed (SP at entry minus lowest SP). The
  observed peak is exact for the paths exercised, and includes libgcc helpers for C and D. It is not a static call-graph
  worst case, though with inlined stages and no recursion the loop is a single frame (208 B) for the fixed-point builds.
* Soft-float numbers are those of GCC's libgcc `fp-bit` soft-float from this toolchain; another soft-float library
  (compiler-rt, Berkeley softfloat, newlib builtins) will differ, and the float source uses the idiomatic float forms for
  inverse sqrt and table indexing, so the "identical structure" is identical at the algorithm level but not instruction for
  instruction. Float has no `sqrtf`/libm dependency.
* Config C uses libgcc's `__muldi3` for rv32i; hand-tuned multiply routines or 32x32 products with a different Q-format
  split could be cheaper. Config C's mul loops are data dependent, so its mean depends on the signal levels in the scenario.
* In the rv32imc builds libgcc helpers are not compressed (the toolchain's rv32imc selects the rv32im libgcc). This does not
  affect the fixed-point builds, which use no helpers.
* Compiler: GCC 13.2.0 only. A different version or newer GCC may differ by a few percent.
* Instruction counts, not cycles: no pipeline, memory-latency or cache model. MIPS here means millions of retired
  instructions per second. See the sensitivity table.
* The scenario is synthetic and open-loop with respect to the actuators (gyro/accel come from a scripted truth, the
  controller outputs do not feed back). The data-dependent branches are exercised by deliberately injected events; real
  flight will saturate more or less often. The effect on the count is small (in config A, min to max spans 14-18% of the mean within a mode, mostly from the saturation/desaturation and clamp paths).
* Per-stage numbers come from a separate noinline build whose total is about 9% above config A; use them for proportions only.

## 7. Files

* `run_all.sh`: everything, one command. `NITER=N ./run_all.sh` changes iterations per mode. `RISCV_PREFIX=...` selects another toolchain prefix.
* `src/`: `control_step.c` (loop), `control.h` (types), `num_fixed.h` / `num_float.h` (numeric backends), `control_init.c`, `harness.c`, `crt0.S`, `link.ld`, `sizes.c`.
* `scenario/`: generators for the input scenario and for the filter/table constants.
* `tools/rvsim.c` (counter), `tools/analyze.py` (tables, report), `tools/qemu_xcheck.sh`, `tools/qemu_count.py`, `tools/coverage.sh`.
* `tests/`: simulator known-answer tests (`gen_kat.py`, `run_kat.sh`).
* `build/`: all generated binaries, per-config `iters.csv` (raw per-iteration counts), symbol maps, `.su` files. `results/`: summaries and logs.
